# Design system: Instrument

Instrument is how ihaveasegway.com looks and moves: a Swiss-style 12-column frame, a poster-size condensed name and small mono labels for dates and figures, in petrol and apricot. Its feel comes from structure and type, not from neon, glitch or sound, and nothing on a page describes how the site is built ([Nothing on the page describes the site](#nothing-on-the-page-describes-the-site)). This doc owns the tokens, the palette and its measured contrast, typography, the motifs, motion, the effects catalog, the design budgets, the accessibility rules and the layout of Home. Why the tokens are a copy owned by this repository, and how the direction was chosen, is [ADR 0010](adr/0010-quiet-console-design-tokens.md). Home's sections and copy belong to [content.md](content.md); the policy that admits the theme script belongs to [security.md](security.md); where each check runs belongs to [delivery.md](delivery.md).

## Chosen direction

Chosen on 2026-10-23 (Q17 in [product.md](product.md#decided)). The exploration drew three directions on Home's wireframe, at 1440 px and 390 px, in both themes: A "Drawing Set", B "Field Notes" and C "Instrument". Joseph picked C, and then Petrol + Apricot from four color studies of it; Graphite + Cobalt, Ink + Chartreuse, Paper + Swiss red, and C's first palette of graphite and orange lost. Cards keep the hover he preferred: a 2 px accent bar that sweeps across the card's top edge, rather than corner crop marks.

What C is, as PR 3 built it and 2026-10-24 left it ([The poster setting](#the-poster-setting), [Nothing on the page describes the site](#nothing-on-the-page-describes-the-site)):

- **A 12-column frame.** The frame is 1,312 px of content inside 64 px margins: `max-width: 90rem` on a border-box element, so the padding counts inside it. That sits inside the 1,200 to 1,320 px most sites hold their content to (GitHub's 1,280 px, Tailwind's `max-w-7xl` 1,280 px, Bootstrap's widest container 1,320 px), and a wider screen gets a wider margin. From 2026-10-26 it stopped at 1,792 px for a day, with the display sizes grown to fill it, after Joseph read the page on a 2,240 px screen as "very narrow"; the same day he read that as too wide, and the frame went back. Phones get 18 px margins. Rows sit on twelve columns, four on a phone; the gutter is `--gutter` on `.frame`, 16 px and 28 px from 64rem. The columns are structure, not a picture: nothing draws them. The numbered ruler that did, across the top of every page, left on 2026-10-24 ([The poster setting](#the-poster-setting)).
- **A poster-size name.** 64 px at 390 px and below, 144 px at 1440 px, a ratio of 2.25. It is set at 80% width on two lines, both starting at the frame's edge. At 64 px "Peterson" needs about 200 px of the 284 px a 320 px screen leaves. The size also stops at 20vw, which never binds at the default text size from 320 px up; with a larger default text size on a phone it keeps "Peterson" whole on the screen ([Type scale](#type-scale)). Beside the name from 75rem, under it before that: the title, the location and the actions.
- **One accent, as punctuation.** Apricot fills the primary button; it draws focus and the card bar, and colors a link on hover. Otherwise links read as text with an underline at 3:1, so the underline, not the color, marks them (SC 1.4.1). `--signal` and `--ring` point at the accent.
- **Type.** Instrument Sans, whose width axis runs from 75% to 100% and gives the condensed name, card titles and page titles, and JetBrains Mono for a post's dates and kind, a card's tag and figures, and code; both OFL 1.1, self-hosted ([Typography](#typography)).
- **Square corners.** `--radius-md` is 0: buttons and code blocks are square, as on a printed sheet. Inline code keeps its 2 px.
- **Cards react only when they lead somewhere.** A card is a row of an index under a rule. A card that holds a link shows the bar over its rule and a stronger rule on hover, and `:focus-within` mirrors it for the keyboard. A card with nothing to activate stays still, because a reaction would promise a click that does nothing. Since 2026-10-24 Home draws no such card: a card renders only with a page to open (`lib/work.ts`), and the stylesheet keeps the rule, which `e2e/motion.spec.ts` checks on a card it adds.

**Icons.** Chosen on 2026-10-23 from five options on the design canvas. The favicon is a generic self-balancing scooter seen from the front: two wheels and a T handlebar, apricot on a petrol tile, legible at 16 px. The 180 px touch icon, which iOS and iMessage show, is a rider on the scooter, which needs about 44 px to read, so tabs keep the front view. Both are drawn for this site, not taken from a maker's logo (Q21 in [product.md](product.md)). `public/favicon.svg` and `apps/web/icons/touch-icon.svg` are the sources; `pnpm --filter web icons` renders `favicon.ico` and `apple-touch-icon.png` from them. The touch icon is a full-bleed square, because iOS rounds the corners itself and fills transparency with black. Its wheel is drawn last, over the rider's leg and the stem, with the tile's petrol inside the ring: drawn first, the leg's 3-unit stroke filled most of the wheel, which read at 180 px as a crescent with a dark sliver beside it (QA, 2026-10-25). `test/touch-icon.test.ts` reads the PNG and requires every pixel inside the ring to be the tile's color, with a known answer.

**Forced colors.** A Windows contrast theme replaces the button's apricot fill with the user's background but keeps the label's own color, which axe measured at 1.02:1. So under `forced-colors: active` the primary button takes system colors (`ButtonFace`, `ButtonText`). The rules between rows are borders, which forced colors draw in the user's text color. System color keywords are the one kind of color allowed outside `tokens.css`. The theme toggle is hidden there: forced colors replace both themes with the visitor's own, so a press would change nothing, and until 2026-10-25 the toggle was shown and focusable anyway (`e2e/theme.spec.ts`).

### The poster setting

Set on 2026-10-24, from Joseph's ask to make the site exceptional and take a few risks, then pared back the same day by his direction that the site stop demonstrating ([next section](#nothing-on-the-page-describes-the-site)). Home reads as a printed poster: the same tokens, faces, rows and checks, with the name set large.

- **The name is the poster.** Two lines at 64→144 px in the condensed face. The `<h1>` holds the two words and nothing else, so its accessible name, its link preview and the smoke check are unchanged. The break comes from the box being as wide as its widest line (`min-content`). The box starts 0.055em left of the frame, the side bearing of "P" in both the web face and the fitted fallback, so the stem of "P" lines up with the frame's edge.
- **Rules, not boxes.** Section titles and the footer hang from rules; cards and posts in a list are rows of an index under a 3:1 rule. What separates things on Home is a rule or a column.
- **Columns as structure.** From 75rem the hero's facts stand in columns 9 to 12 (from 64rem until 2026-10-25, but below about 70rem the title needs two lines there); a card or a post in a list puts its tag or date in columns 1 and 2, its title in 3 to 8 and its small print in 9 to 12; a long-form page's head puts its label in columns 1 and 2 and the rest from column 3. On a phone each stacks.
- **What left on 2026-10-24, and why.** Each of these showed how the page was built, or decorated it, and none told a visitor anything about Joseph:
  - the ruler, a numbered tick band over twelve columns on every page and on the link preview image: the layout grid on display;
  - the step, "Joseph" indented by one column with an apricot field in the step: a stranger read the field as a missing photo or logo;
  - section numbers and How I work's numerals: numbering for its own sake, which stacked two "01"s when one section rendered;
  - How I work, four statements on a full-width apricot field: the site's own build rules restated as principles, with no evidence, given the most weight on the page;
  - the square location mark, the `hello@` hint on the Email button, the footer's "Contact" label and its address set at 24→60 px, the "Error 404" label, and the toggle's words ("Theme: system", and "saved in this browser" after a press);
  - the domain as the header's wordmark: the header names Joseph instead, on every page but Home, where the `<h1>` does.
- **What it cost.** Nothing added: the theme script lost 243 B (789 → 546 B gzip -9) and CSS went from 6,686 to 5,916 B. The fold moved from 459.1 to 368.2 px of 844 on a phone and from 541.0 to 420.7 px of 900 on a laptop ([Budgets](#budgets-as-ci-fitness-functions)).
- **What would show it was wrong.** A recruiter shown Home for 7 seconds who cannot recall the name, or reviewers calling Home unfinished while it holds the hero alone. The second is a content gap, not a layout one: Selected work and Writing render as soon as a post is published or a project's checks pass.

### Nothing on the page describes the site

Set on 2026-10-24, from Joseph's direction: "There is a difference between demonstrating and executing." The site is for a visitor who wants to know who Joseph is, see his work, read his writing and reach him. So nothing on any page exists to show how the site is built.

- **What left.** Home's readout row (Build, Built, JavaScript), the footer's build line on every other page, and the "This site" card with its JavaScript and fonts figures. No page prints a commit, a build date or a figure about the site's own bytes, and none ever draws a gauge or a meter. The served-from readout planned for PR 4 is dropped with them. A second pass the same day took out the decoration that showed the grid or the build ([The poster setting](#the-poster-setting)), the comments in the inline theme script and in `robots.txt`, which every visitor's browser downloads, and the build's commit from every page's markup.
- **What stays, unprinted.** The byte budgets are enforced in CI exactly as before (`test/budgets.test.ts`, through `src/lib/weights.ts`). Which commit is live is a machine's question: the build adds `X-Build: <full commit>` to the `/*` rule of `dist/_headers` (`src/lib/build.mjs`, through `astro.config.mjs`), so every response carries it as a header. The deploy smoke reads it (`e2e/smoke.spec.ts`); no page holds it.
- **Checked.** `test/readouts.test.ts` requires `_headers` to send the commit the checkout builds, once, under `/*`, finds the commit in no page and in no other file the build writes, and holds Home and the 404 to no byte figure, no build readout and no "This site" card, with known answers. `e2e/fold.spec.ts` expects the hero alone above the fold.
- **The rule for what comes next.** A new element earns its place by telling the visitor something about Joseph or his work. One that tells them about the site is left out, however true it is.

## Why

- Recruiters give the page seconds and engineers give it a minute ([content.md](content.md) has the evidence). A slow, hard-to-read or templated page contradicts what the site says about its author, so speed and accessibility are requirements here, not polish.
- The ask was a "cyber" feel. The need is the work. Structure earns its place only where it helps a reader find it: the grid orders the page and the type carries the name, the work and the writing. The site's own engineering is checked by machines and not shown (principle 6 in [product.md](product.md), as revised on 2026-10-24).
- If nothing is done, the site gets shadcn's stock look, which reads as a template, or a sci-fi skin that delays the first word.

### Measured contrast

Method: OKLCH to OKLab to linear sRGB ([Ottosson's matrices](https://bottosson.github.io/posts/oklab/)), then WCAG 2.2 [relative luminance](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#dfn-relative-luminance), with [culori](https://culorijs.org/api/) 4.0.2. Every token is inside the sRGB gamut, so no browser clips it. `test/tokens.test.ts` computes the full cross product from `tokens.css` on every pull request: every text token against every surface at 4.5:1, every non-text token at 3:1, and the label on each accent fill. The values below are from 2026-10-27, dark / light.

| Token | Need | on background | on surface-1 | on surface-2 | on ground |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| foreground (also `destructive`, for now) | 4.5 | 14.92 / 15.21 | 13.29 / 14.21 | 11.65 / 13.29 | 12.08 / 13.09 |
| muted-foreground | 4.5 | 8.98 / 6.35 | 8.00 / 5.93 | 7.02 / 5.55 | 7.28 / 5.46 |
| subtle-foreground | 4.5 | 6.78 / 5.82 | 6.04 / 5.43 | 5.30 / 5.08 | 5.49 / 5.01 |
| primary (also `signal` and the focus `ring`) | 4.5 | 9.99 / 5.78 | 8.90 / 5.40 | 7.81 / 5.05 | 8.10 / 4.97 |
| primary-strong | 4.5 | 11.46 / 7.47 | 10.21 / 6.97 | 8.96 / 6.52 | 9.28 / 6.42 |
| input (borders, underlines) | 3.0 | 4.27 / 4.05 | 3.80 / 3.79 | 3.34 / 3.54 | 3.46 / 3.49 |

On the accent fills: `primary-foreground` on `primary` measures 10.91 / 6.41, and on `primary-strong` 12.51 / 8.28. `border` is decoration and carries no claim (2.09 / 1.34 on the background); it is still checked for gamut. `ground` is a surface, so every text and non-text token is held to it: its lines are faint (1.23 / 1.16 on the background), and a word that crosses one keeps its contrast.

Every pair passes in both themes, and the test lists no forbidden pair. The tightest text pairs are 5.30:1 in dark (`subtle-foreground` on `surface-2`) and 4.97:1 in light (`primary` on `ground`); the tightest non-text pair is `input` on `surface-2` in dark, 3.34:1. The test also fails when a color token has no declared role, and when the light block for a stored choice and the light block for the OS differ.

**Selected text.** Every `::selection` rule, in `global.css` or the Route Lab's own sheet, sets both a text color and a fill, each a token, so the pair holds whatever color the text had: `primary-foreground` on `primary`, 10.91 / 5.65, and on the Email button, whose label already sits on that fill, `background` on `foreground`, 14.92 / 16.39. Display type, every heading and the title under the name, is the one exception, and sets no fill: most of it sets its lines closer together than the face is tall (0.86 for the name, 1.02 to 1.2 for titles), and a browser paints each line's fill over the line before it, so the first fix of 2026-10-25, a fill everywhere, hid the foot of the line above: with the name selected, "Joseph" read as "Iosenh". Selected display type turns `primary` on the surface it sits on instead, 9.99 / 5.49 on the background and 7.81 / 4.66 on `surface-2`; the Route Lab's display type, its headline and each plan's time, set at 1.15 and 1.02, does the same. Its maps are pictures, whose facts the tables carry: their labels, numbers and tip cannot be selected (`user-select: none`), since a fill under every label hid the routes and stops beneath it, and the labels, drawn once in hidden defs, set no fill either. The chips for the vans set their mono numbers at a line height of 1.4, more than the face is tall, where 1.25 was less. Until 2026-10-25 the selection was a 30% apricot tint over the page and the text kept its own color: muted text on it measured 4.41 / 4.11 over the background and 3.61 / 3.59 over `surface-2`, under 4.5:1 in both themes. `test/tokens.test.ts` holds every `::selection` rule in every stylesheet to 4.5:1 in both themes, a rule with no fill on every surface, and keeps the old tint as a known answer; `e2e/a11y.spec.ts` finds any text, on every page, whose block sets its lines closer than its face is tall and whose selection still has a fill, with known answers. The lesson Quiet Console's audit taught stands: check the cross product, not a hand-picked list, because a fitness function only guards what it declares.

### Map colors (the Route Lab)

Set on 2026-10-25 with the Route Lab ([ADR 0016](adr/0016-route-lab-in-the-browser.md)), and revised the same day after the reviews found the bands too close to tell apart. A map needs more than one accent: four vans share two maps, and a reader has to tell their routes apart at a glance. So the map has its own tokens, used by the Route Lab's page and nowhere else, and the accent stays the site's one accent everywhere else. ## Tokens (CSS)

`apps/web/src/styles/tokens.css` is the only file in `apps/web/src` that may hold a color value; `test/color-literals.test.ts` fails on a literal anywhere else, system color keywords excepted. This section explains the file rather than copying it.

- **Two tiers.** Tier 1 (primitives) are the `oklch()` literals, and they live only in the theme blocks. Tier 2 (semantic) uses shadcn's names, and components use only these, through Tailwind (`bg-background`, `text-muted-foreground`) or `var()`. Tier 3 (component) tokens narrow a semantic token for one component; none exist since the corner brackets and the terminal panel left. The weaker design lets a component carry its own literal: then a theme change becomes a search across every file, and the contrast test cannot see the value.
- **Tailwind's own scales are removed** (`--color-*`, `--text-*`, `--radius-*` and `--font-*` set to `initial`), so a stray utility class produces nothing instead of an untested color. `@theme inline` maps the semantic tokens to Tailwind's names.
- **Themes.** Dark is the designed default: `:root` and `:root[data-theme='dark']` share one block. A stored light choice has its own block, and the OS's light preference has a second copy under `prefers-color-scheme: light`, scoped with `:where()` so a stored dark choice still wins. The test fails if the two light copies differ.
- **More contrast.** Under `prefers-contrast: more`, `subtle-foreground` steps up to `muted-foreground` and `border` to `input`, over any stored theme.
- **Print.** Paper is white whatever the screen showed: the print block sets the surfaces to white, text to black and the accent to ink. Since 2026-10-25 it sets the hairlines too, to a neutral gray a printer keeps (2.2:1 on white), and underlines and fields to 4.9:1; before, they kept the screen theme's, near-white from light and dark gray from dark. The primary button prints as an outline with ink text, so it reads the same whether or not the browser prints backgrounds. Paper gets no control: the theme toggle, the skip link and the nav are not printed, nor is Home's header, which holds nothing else. A link whose words are not its address prints the address after it in small mono, the site's own with the domain, so a post's citations survive on paper; in running text the address flows with the words. A button prints no address: the footer prints the same ones. Inline code drops its padding on paper and in forced colors, where it read as a stray space. `e2e/print.spec.ts` checks every page on the site and on the writing fixture. The résumé has print rules of its own, on one named page ([The résumé](#the-résumé)).
- **Specificity.** The more-contrast and print blocks name `:root[data-theme]` too, so they outrank every theme block, stored or not. A bare `:root` would lose to `[data-theme]`, and a stored dark theme would print near-white text.

One alternative was considered and deferred: `light-dark()` with `color-scheme: light dark` would follow the OS with no copy of the light values, and would define each token once. It reaches Baseline widely available around 2026-11 (newly available on 2024-05-13), and a browser without it would get no colors at all, while the stored choice still needs the pre-paint script. Revisit when the token file is next restructured.

Browser support, from the [web-features](https://github.com/web-platform-dx/web-features) Baseline data as of 2026-10-22: `oklch()` and `color-mix()` are Baseline widely available (since 2025-11-09); cross-document view transitions (Chrome 126, Safari 18.2) are not Baseline because Firefox lacks them, which is why they sit behind a working default; `:has()`, which the card's hover uses, is newly available, and the keyboard state does not depend on it.

## Theme before first paint

The theme must be set before the first paint, or a visitor whose stored choice differs from the OS sees one frame in the wrong theme. `src/scripts/theme.js` does it, inlined in `<head>`:

- **What it sets.** With `dark` or `light` stored for this tab, `data-theme` takes that value. Otherwise `data-theme="system"`, and CSS treats `system` exactly like a missing attribute: both follow the OS.
- **The back-forward cache.** A page the browser restores from its back-forward cache comes back as the visitor left it, and the script does not run again, so a choice made on another page in between would not show: QA saw the old theme on a restored page while `sessionStorage` held the new one (2026-10-25). The script listens for `pageshow` and, when `event.persisted` says the page came from the cache, applies the stored choice again. When storage refuses to be read, the restored page keeps the choice it shows, since a choice storage refused holds for the page it was made on.
- **The fail-safe is the OS.** No stored choice, storage that throws (private modes, blocked storage), a blocked script and no JavaScript at all share one path: the page follows `prefers-color-scheme`. Storage access sits in `try`/`catch`.
- **Where it runs.** In `<head>`, right after the `color-scheme` and `theme-color` metas and before any stylesheet, so it has always run before the first frame that can paint `<body>`, however the HTML arrives. PR 3 first placed it as the first child of `<body>`, and the first Worker Preview showed the gap: when the HTML arrived in pieces, a frame painted before the script ran.
- **It is governed.** The page policy is a header the build writes, with a hash of each inline script a page holds, this one among them, so it applies before the first byte of markup ([security.md](security.md#headers-and-content-security-policy), [ADR 0018](adr/0018-page-policy-as-a-header.md)). An edit cannot leave a stale hash behind: the build hashes what the page holds. `test/csp.test.ts` requires the script to be the first, in `<head>`, byte for byte the file, and an e2e test places a copy with one byte changed first in `<head>` and requires the browser to refuse it. Until 2026-10-25 the policy was Astro's `<meta>` at the end of `<head>`, which covers only what follows it, so this one script ran ungoverned, an exception those two checks pinned.
- **The browser chrome follows the page.** Two `<meta name="theme-color">` tags, one per OS scheme (`media="(prefers-color-scheme: dark)"` and `light`), carry the `background` token of their theme as `#rrggbb`, resolved from `tokens.css` at build (`chromeColor` in `src/lib/tokens.ts`), so `src/` holds no color literal and a palette change moves the bar above the page with it. Without JavaScript they follow the OS, as the page does. A stored theme would leave the bar on the OS's color, so the script, at load and on every press, gives both metas the stored theme's color, and System gives each its own back. They come before the script, which finds them before the first paint. `test/theme-color.test.ts` pins both values to the token on every page of every build, with their order; `e2e/theme.spec.ts` requires the color a browser would take from them to be the body's painted background in every case below.
- **`theme` is the only key the site stores, and only for the tab** (`sessionStorage`, [security.md](security.md#privacy-and-data-inventory)). A choice holds across the pages of a visit and a reload; a new tab starts from the OS again. Until 2026-10-24 the choice went to `localStorage`, which kept it across visits but needed the toggle to say "saved in this browser", the site explaining its own storage on every press. An e2e test reads both stores after the toggle has run.
- **The toggle** is an icon button at the end of the header, and the same script handles its clicks, so it adds no script of its own. It cycles System, then the theme the OS is not showing, then the one it is, and back to System: on a dark OS, System, Light, Dark. Every press but the return to System changes the page, so a first press never looks broken. Each state has its own icon, which CSS picks from `data-theme`: a half-filled circle for System, a sun for Light, a moon for Dark. So every press changes what the toggle shows, the return to System included; until 2026-10-25 one icon served all three, and on a dark OS the press from Dark to System changed nothing visible. Its name, "Theme: system", "Theme: light" or "Theme: dark", is in the one of three state spans CSS leaves displayed, clipped so only assistive technology reads it, and the span carries a `title` with the same words over the whole button, for a pointer. It is 44 × 44 px. Without JavaScript nothing sets `data-theme`, so the toggle stays hidden, and print and forced colors hide it too: there is never a control that does nothing. If storage refuses a choice, it holds for the page.
- **No comments ship.** The script is inlined as written, byte for byte the file the CSP hash is computed from, so what it does is told here and in `Base.astro`, not in the file.
- **Size.** The script measured 624 B gzip on 2026-10-23, against the 0.3 KB the plan estimated for the toggle alone. Keeping the browser chrome in step added 165 B: 789 B gzip (1,669 B as written) on 2026-10-24. Without its comments and the saved flag, the same day: 546 B gzip (1,185 B as written). Applying the choice again when the back-forward cache restores a page, and keeping the page's own choice there when storage refuses, added 45 B: 591 B gzip (1,356 B as written) on 2026-10-25. It was the only JavaScript on a content page until the command menu's module joined it on every page, the same day ([The command menu](#the-command-menu)).
- **Tested** (`e2e/theme.spec.ts`): the first frame that can paint `<body>` already has the right `data-theme`, background and browser-chrome color in eight cases, a dark or light OS with nothing, light, dark or throwing storage chosen; the cycle on both OSes, where every press but the last changes the page, the chrome follows each press, `theme` is the only key in `sessionStorage` and `localStorage` stays empty; a choice survives a reload, a new tab starts from the OS, and refused storage still applies the choice to the page; System follows an OS change live, chrome included; a chosen theme of either kind prints black on white, the Email button included, with the toggle hidden; more contrast applies over a chosen theme; and without JavaScript the page and its chrome follow the OS and show no toggle. Since 2026-10-25: every press changes the toggle's picture, and the title under a pointer anywhere on it says what its name says; forced colors hide it; and `e2e/bfcache.spec.ts`, with the back-forward cache on (Playwright's Chromium turns it off, and its headless shell never restores from it, so that file runs full Chromium), requires a page restored from the cache to show a choice made after leaving it, and to keep a choice storage refused, and proves each time that the page came from the cache.

- **Cut from pinned sources.** `apps/web/scripts/fonts/build.sh` downloads each source into a fresh temporary directory, refuses any file whose size or sha256 differs from `fonts.lock.json` before a font parser reads it, and cuts with fontTools 4.67.0 from hash-pinned wheels in a throwaway environment. CI never runs it: `test/fonts.test.ts` checks every committed and served font against the lock, byte for byte, and checks each file's name strings and tables, with known answers.
- **Code ligatures stay out.** `calt` and `liga` would draw `=>` as an arrow, which misreads in code, and cost bytes.
- **Delivery (spike S9, passed).** Astro's Fonts API with the local provider serves both files from `/_astro/fonts/`, passes the width range through to `font-stretch`, writes a preload link for each face that asks for one, and emits a `<style>` with the faces, which the build hashes into the page policy's `style-src` ([roadmap.md](roadmap.md#spikes), [ADR 0018](adr/0018-page-policy-as-a-header.md)). Its own fallback is one face fitted to Arial at full width, which the condensed name needs per width, so it is off.
- **Fitted fallback faces.** `src/styles/font-fallbacks.css`, written by `build.sh`, holds one fallback family per common local font: Arial (and Liberation Sans and Arimo, which share its widths), Roboto for Android, DejaVu Sans for Linux without Liberation, and for mono Menlo or DejaVu Sans Mono and Courier New or Cousine. Each has `size-adjust` and ascent, descent and line-gap overrides fitted to the web font: the 100% faces to English letter frequencies, the 75–85% faces to "Joseph Peterson", at 80%. Since 2026-10-24 all display type is set at that width, the name and card titles on Home and the titles of every other page, so the fitted faces stand in for all of it: a statement measured 3.3% narrower in the fallback than in the web face at 80%, against 9% at 85%. Text breaks in the same place before and after the web font arrives; the fold check measures the hero's height both ways.
- **Link previews.** Satori draws a variable font at its default instance and ignores `font-stretch`, so `build.sh` also cuts two static Instrument Sans instances for the Open Graph card (`src/og/fonts/`). They never reach the browser.

Self-hosting is not only about bytes. Since Chrome 86 the HTTP cache is partitioned per site ([Chrome](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/http-cache-partitioning)), so a shared Google Fonts cache gives no benefit. A third-party font also needs its own connection setup ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/font-best-practices)), and every request to it hands the visitor's IP address to another company.

Rules:

- **Mono for values, never prose.** Card facts, a card's tag, a post's dates and kind, and code are mono; sentences, the location line, the copyright and, since 2026-10-25, the arrow on a button are sans, so a page with no values fetches no mono face.
- Fluid sizes mix `rem` and `vw`, each has a `rem` floor, and each maximum is at most 2.5 times its minimum (the hero: 2.25), so 200% zoom still enlarges text (SC 1.4.4; Maxwell Barvian, ["Addressing Accessibility Concerns With Using Fluid Type"](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2023/11/addressing-accessibility-concerns-fluid-type/), 2023-11-07). `test/tokens.test.ts` checks all three.
- **A cap in vw, only where it never binds at the default size.** A fluid size may also stop at a width, `min(<clamp>, <n>vw)`, when n vw at 320 px is at least what the clamp gives there and n is at least the clamp's own vw rate: at the default text size the cap then never binds from 320 px up, and zoom enlarges the text exactly as without it. The hero has one, 20vw, its 64 px floor at 320 px, set on 2026-10-25 after QA found the name broken as "Peterso / n" with the browser's text at 24 px and 32 px on a phone. The cap binds only there, a larger default text size on a narrow screen, where the name could not grow without breaking inside a word or scrolling sideways; the name is then as large as the screen allows, 64 px at 320 px and 78 px at 390 px. `test/tokens.test.ts` checks the cap too, and still refuses PR 2's 16.5vw cap, which bound below 400 px at the default size.
- Numbers that line up (dates, figures in a table) use tabular numerals.
- **The measure.** Body text runs about 68 characters a line: `--measure: 31em` in `tokens.css`. Not `68ch`: `ch` is the width of the digit zero, which Instrument Sans draws wide, so a 68ch column measured 95 to 103 characters a line of English at 16 px, past the 80 that SC 1.4.8 asks for. At 31em the same paragraph runs 62 to 71 (2026-10-23), and `e2e/writing.spec.ts` counts them in the browser.

## Motifs

The feel comes from structure. Each motif is CSS or real text, and each one survives with JavaScript off.

- **The name.** Two lines in the condensed face, the size of a poster's headline ([The poster setting](#the-poster-setting)).
- **The card bar.** The 2 px apricot bar that sweeps across a card's rule when the card leads somewhere ([Chosen direction](#chosen-direction)).
- **Timestamps.** ISO 8601 dates, with no time of day and no zone: midnight UTC carries neither, and anything else would tell a reader when or where the build ran. A post's dates are the only dates a page shows. `test/readouts.test.ts` scans every served file for a time of day or an offset in every common form. No ticking clock: a clock is auto-updating content under SC 2.2.2, and it says nothing about the work.
- **Removed: coordinates and a fixed UTC offset.** The research wireframe put one-decimal coordinates and a UTC offset in the hero label. Both are gone. One decimal of latitude and longitude resolves to about 11 km, which is city scale; next to a title, years and an industry, that narrows the search to one employer, which is the mosaic effect the content firewall exists to prevent ([ADR 0007](adr/0007-content-firewall.md)). A fixed offset is also wrong for half the year: a US offset moves by one hour at each daylight saving change (the next one is 2026-11-01). The hero carries only the location line Joseph chose (Q5 · Q-location in [product.md](product.md)).
- **Page geometry (2026-10-27).** Each section draws its own fine lines behind the page's top right ([Page geometry](#page-geometry)).
- **Removed on 2026-10-24:** the ruler, the step and its field, section numbers, the location mark and How I work's field ([The poster setting](#the-poster-setting)).

| 404 | a field of stops, a route that turns back short, and the one stop it never reaches as the accent mark | VROOM's "unassigned": the page no route reaches |
| Résumé | none | it is read and printed, not browsed |

**How it is made.** `src/lib/ground.ts` generates each drawing at build from the section and the page's path, through a seeded generator, so the same page always draws the same lines and its markup changes only when the drawing does. `components/Ground.astro` writes it as an inline SVG in `<body>`, before the masthead: hidden from assistive technology, no pointer events, classes and never style attributes (the page policy refuses those), and hidden in print. It sits at its natural size behind the top right from 75rem, cut by the window below that, and two masks fade it toward the text column and toward the fold. Nothing moves.

**Why generated and not traced from the map.** The Route Lab's isochrones and streets come from OpenStreetMap. Drawn on every page, they would be a produced work under the ODbL, and every page would need its notice. They would also cost about 30 KB gzip of map data where the generated drawing costs about 2 KB. A generated drawing has neither cost and looks the same at this weight.

**Contrast.** Every line is `--ground`, a token the contrast test treats as a surface, so every text token reads on it at 4.5:1 and a word that crosses a line keeps its contrast ([Measured contrast](#measured-contrast)). The one accent mark is `--primary`, which is not a surface, so `e2e/ground.spec.ts` requires every mark to touch no text on every kind of page at 320, 390, 768 and 1440 px, with a known answer that moves a mark onto the name.

**Weight.** At most 2,500 B gzip of path data per drawing, in `test/ground.test.ts`. Home's contours are the heaviest, at 2,318 B: 13 lines of 72 points each. Every coordinate is a whole number.

**Weaker designs.** A CSS background image would be cached, but it cannot read the page's color tokens, so it would need one file per theme and a mask to recolor it. A canvas or WebGL drawing would cost JavaScript and battery on every visit for a picture that never changes. Real map data would bring the license notice and the bytes. A drawing under the whole page would sit under body text, where the faint lines would compete with reading.

## Motion

| Kind | Duration and easing | With `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` |
|---|---|---|
| Card bar sweep | 220 ms, ease-out, `transform` only | the bar appears at once |
| A card's or a post's title rising out of its baseline as it scrolls into view (2026-10-26; not on the résumé, not in print) | scroll-driven (`animation-timeline: view()`), linear over the first 15% to 85% of its entry, `transform` and `clip-path` only, never opacity | none: the title is in place |
| Hover color, focus | instant | the same |

| A section's rule drawing itself in from the left as it scrolls into view (2026-10-27; not on the résumé, not in print, not in a contrast theme) | scroll-driven, on the rising titles' range, `transform` only | none: the rule is drawn |
| Theme switch | instant | the same |
| "Play the day" on the Route Lab's space-time cube (2026-10-26): the time level climbs through both cubes, started and paused by the visitor, stopped at the day's end | the whole time axis in 12 s, linear, drawn in WebGL; no CSS animation | steps half an hour a second instead of gliding |
| The Raft Lab's run (2026-10-27): messages glide between the five servers and each election timer's dial fills, started and paused by the visitor, paused when the tab is hidden, frozen when a safety property breaks ([ADR 0020](adr/0020-raft-lab-deterministic-simulation.md)) | at 1/40, 1/20 or 1/10 of virtual time, drawn in JavaScript on animation frames; no CSS animation | each message stands still half way along its path, and Run advances 50 virtual ms every half second |
| The Route Lab's maps, when a plan changes or goes stale (2026-10-25): VROOM's map dims while it plans changed orders again, and clears when it answers | 220 ms, ease-out, `opacity` only | the map dims or clears at once |

Rules:

- **Author motion inside `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`** (no-motion-first). State changes live outside the query, so a reduced-motion visitor still sees the card's bar; only the movement is removed. `test/tokens.test.ts` fails if a transition, an animation, smooth scrolling or a view transition appears anywhere else.
- **Animate `transform` and `opacity`.** Never animate layout properties such as width, height, margin or top.
- **Nothing moves for more than 5 s without a pause control** (SC 2.2.2). **Nothing flashes more than 3 times a second** (SC 2.3.1).
- **No motion on body copy, and never on the LCP element.** The hero name is the likely LCP element; it is never faded or delayed.
- **Nothing a pointer moves may shift the layout.** A pointer's movement is not an input that excuses a shift, so a shift it causes counts against CLS in the field, where no lab test hovers. That is why the hero name does not tune its width axis to the pointer ([Deferred](#deferred)), and why a decoding tag cycles only capitals and digits in a mono face.
- **One moving thing on screen at a time. No bounce or elastic easing.** A scroll-driven reveal moves only while the visitor scrolls, and runs back when they scroll up, so it is the scroll that moves, not the page.
- **Never fade text in.** A title half way through a fade is half transparent, under 4.5:1, and axe reads it that way; a reveal moves and clips instead.
- **Keyboard-driven UI opens instantly, and frequent actions animate less** (Rauno Freiberg, see [Prior art](#prior-art)). The command menu has no open animation: it is there on the press.

## One nav manifest

One list feeds every way of getting around: `apps/web/src/lib/nav.ts`. The weaker design gives the header, the footer, the command menu and the sitemap their own lists; the third copy drifts the first time a page is renamed.

- **Writer.** Joseph writes the list, by pull request: each item has an id, a label, an `href`, and whether it belongs in the header, the footer or both. The build keeps only the items whose page it writes, so a link to a page that does not exist cannot render. Static pages are read from `src/pages`; Writing's indexes and feed are dynamic routes, so their URLs come from the posts (`lib/posts.ts`, `areaRoutes`). The rule "a section shows only when it has content" ([ADR 0005](adr/0005-url-scheme.md), [content.md](content.md)) holds by construction rather than by review.
- **Today.** In order: Lab, Writing, Résumé, About, RSS and Colophon. Résumé (2026-10-25) is a page file, so every build writes it and the header links it. Lab appears with the first live demo (the Route Lab, so since 2026-10-25), and Writing (the header) and RSS (the footer) with the first published post. About (PR 4) and the Colophon (PR 3b) are listed and not written yet. Work left the manifest on 2026-10-23, when owned work became cards on Home. "Résumé" is set in the sans face, which has é; the mono subset does not, so no mono label may hold it. ## Long-form pages (PR 5)

Posts now, and the colophon with its decision records in PR 3b, share one layout: `layouts/Prose.astro`, with `components/PageHead.astro` for the head and `components/PostList.astro` for the lists on Home, `/writing/` and `/notes/`. The layout knows nothing about posts: a page passes in its words and puts its body in the slot.

- **Head, on Home's axis.** A mono label (the kind, such as "Essay", with "Draft" before it on a preview's draft page, or an index's post count), the page's one `<h1>` in the condensed face at the statement size, the summary as the lead, and a mono line of dates and tags. From 64rem the label stands in columns 1 and 2 and the rest starts in column 3, as a post's title does in a list; the body, the lists and the links after them start there too. The 404 takes the same head without a label, from the frame's edge. `test/headings.test.ts` holds every page to one `<h1>`, read from the rendered HTML, so no way of writing a heading in a body gets around it.
- **Body.** Markdown as written, at the base size and the measure. Tailwind's preflight removes every margin, list marker and heading size, so `.prose` sets them once: the h2 and h3 sizes, list markers, a quotation's 3:1 rule, and code in the mono face on `surface-1`.
- **Code wraps.** A code block wraps its lines rather than scrolling. A scrolling block would have to take keyboard focus (axe's `scrollable-region-focusable`), and at 320 px it would scroll sideways (SC 1.4.10). There is no syntax highlighting yet: Shiki writes inline styles the policy would refuse, and Expressive Code arrives with the first post that needs it ([stack.md](stack.md)).
- **Tables.** They fill the column at the small size, with tabular figures and hairline rules, and wrap their cells, so a table never scrolls sideways at 320 px. Column alignment is not supported: Markdown writes it as a `style` attribute, which the policy refuses, and `test/csp.test.ts` names it.
- **Footnotes.** As the processor writes them: a section after the body at the small size, above a hairline, and its heading for assistive technology only (`.sr-only`, the same rule as Tailwind's utility, which the build cannot see in the processor's output). The way back is "↑", not the processor's "↩", which neither subset carries (`astro.config.mjs`).
- **Lists.** A post is its date and kind in mono, its title as a link in the condensed face, and its summary: a row of an index under a 3:1 rule, like a card on Home. From 64rem the date and kind stand in columns 1 and 2, the title in 3 to 8 and the summary in 9 to 12; on a phone they stack. Newest first; a day's posts by slug, so the order never depends on the disk.
- **Not yet.** Images in posts. `test/dist-files.test.ts` admits PNG, ICO and WOFF2 binaries only, Astro writes a post's images as WebP, and its asset pipeline emits a draft's images in a production build, so images need a WebP check, an image budget and drafts kept out of the asset pipeline first. Until then `test/writing.test.ts` refuses a Markdown image in a post's source, with that reason, and `test/prose.test.ts` an `img`, `picture` or `source` inside a post's rendered `div.prose`, on every build a Vitest project reads. Raw HTML is not refused yet, and can show an image another way.

## The command menu

Shipped on 2026-10-25, from PR 4's plan: `components/Palette.astro` writes it into every page, and `src/scripts/palette.js`, one module with no imports, opens it. ⌘K or Ctrl+K opens and closes it, never a bare key; so does the footer's button, which shows under a pointer that can hover, once the script has bound the menu (until then it holds its place unseen, so nothing moves), and never without JavaScript or on a touch screen, where the nav, the toggle and the footer hold all it offers. It names the keys this keyboard has: "⌘K" on an Apple one, "Ctrl K" elsewhere, in mono, the face with ⌘.

- **What it is.** A native `<dialog>` opened with `showModal()`, so the page behind it is inert, Esc closes it, and focus returns to whatever opened it. In it, a labelled combobox, "Find a page or an action", and a listbox; `aria-activedescendant` follows the selected row, which `aria-selected` marks, alone. It opens in 0 ms, with no animation, near the top of the screen, where an on-screen keyboard does not cover it.
- **Its rows.** Grouped as Go to, Actions and Links. Go to is Home and the manifest's pages this build writes ([One nav manifest](#one-nav-manifest)); Actions copy the email address and press the theme toggle, whose names the entry shows; Links are the profiles whose readiness check is recorded. Every row exists elsewhere on the page too, so the menu is a shortcut, never the only way in ([content.md](content.md#sitemap-and-phases)); copying the address is the one action it adds.
- **Finding a row.** Typing narrows the rows by subsequence over each label, the value beside it and each keyword, each on its own, without case or accents, so "rsm" and "cv" both find Résumé. A group with no row left is hidden, and the first match is selected, so Enter acts at once. ↑ and ↓ move the selection and wrap; Enter or a click acts, and with ⌘ or Ctrl held a page opens in a new tab. A click on the backdrop closes the menu. What a visitor types is never written back into the page.
- **What it says.** The key help, in mono: "↑/↓ move · enter open · esc close", with "enter" spelled out because `↵` is not in JetBrains Mono. Copying the address shows "Copied" on a line under the rows, and one `role="status"` region inside the dialog, hidden on screen, repeats it (SC 4.1.3): the page behind a modal dialog is inert, so a region outside it would say nothing. The same region gives the count of rows once typing pauses, "No matches" when none is left, and the theme's new name. When the browser refuses the copy, the line shows the address to copy by hand.
- **Where it stops.** Nothing is stored: the theme entry presses the toggle, whose handler is the only code that reads or writes the stored choice. In forced colors the theme entry is gone, as the toggle is. The page the browser keeps for Back never comes back with the menu open. Paper gets no button.
- **Its cost.** 1,519 B gzip of eager JavaScript on every page, against the research figure of 1.18 KB; its markup adds about 640 to 700 B gzip to each page's HTML, and its styles 468 B to the stylesheet. Checked by `test/palette.test.ts` (the key handler and the matcher, with known answers; on every page of every build, the dialog, its rows against the manifest and the checked profiles, the combobox's wiring, the button), `test/scripts.test.ts` (its module once on every page, in `<body>`, from `/_astro/`) and `e2e/palette.spec.ts` (each key, the button, the narrowing, the arrows, each action, the backdrop, touch and no JavaScript, axe in light, dark and forced colors with it open on every page, no policy violation and no animation, and its fit at 320 px and 32 px text). VoiceOver and NVDA over it are Joseph's to run, as over the toggle ([Accessibility rules](#accessibility-rules)).

## Patterns and further reading

Each pattern also has one canonical source in [reading-list.md](reading-list.md).

- Grid systems: Josef Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design* (Niggli, 1981).
- Three-tier design tokens (primitive, semantic, component): Nathan Curtis, ["Naming Tokens in Design Systems"](https://nathanacurtis.substack.com/p/naming-tokens-in-design-systems-9e86c7444676).
- OKLab and OKLCH, perceptual color: Björn Ottosson, ["A perceptual color space for image processing"](https://bottosson.github.io/posts/oklab/); Evil Martians, ["OKLCH in CSS: why we moved from RGB and HSL"](https://evilmartians.com/chronicles/oklch-in-css-why-quit-rgb-hsl).
- Accessible color systems: Stripe, ["Designing accessible color systems"](https://stripe.com/blog/accessible-color-systems).
- No-motion-first: Tatiana Mac, ["prefers-reduced-motion: Taking a no-motion-first approach to animations"](https://www.tatianamac.com/posts/prefers-reduced-motion).
- Motion durations: NN/g, ["Executing UX Animations: Duration and Motion Characteristics"](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration/).
- Progressive enhancement (view transitions and the theme toggle sit on a page that works without them): Jeremy Keith, [*Resilient Web Design*](https://resilientwebdesign.com/).
- Fitness functions: Neal Ford, Rebecca Parsons, Patrick Kua and Pramod Sadalage, [*Building Evolutionary Architectures*, 2nd ed., ch. 2](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/building-evolutionary-architectures/9781492097532/ch02.html).
- Fail-safe default (the OS's theme if the script fails; a row or a field left out if its value cannot be read): Jerome Saltzer and Michael Schroeder, ["The Protection of Information in Computer Systems"](https://web.mit.edu/Saltzer/www/publications/protection/Basic.html) (1975).
- One owner per piece of data (tokens in one file, navigation in one manifest, byte figures in one module, theme choice in the visitor's tab): Andrew Hunt and David Thomas, *The Pragmatic Programmer*, 20th anniversary ed., "DRY: The Evils of Duplication".
- Mosaic effect (why the coordinates and the UTC offset are gone): US Office of Management and Budget, [Memorandum M-13-13](https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/sites/default/files/omb/memoranda/2013/m-13-13.pdf) (2013).
- WCAG 2.2: the W3C Understanding documents linked in [Accessibility rules](#accessibility-rules).
