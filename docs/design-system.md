# Design system: the signal box

The signal box is how ihaveasegway.com looks and moves since 2026-10-30 ([ADR 0025](adr/0025-signal-box-identity.md)). The site is Joseph's signal box: each lab is a line in its own colour, drawn as the real shape of the system it simulates; state is a lamp's shape and a word; type is signage, in Barlow's three widths; and Home's first screen is the Route Lab's day, live on Seattle, with the page itself as the water. Its feel comes from the work drawn as lines, not from neon, glitch or sound, and nothing on a page describes how the site is built ([Nothing on the page describes the site](#nothing-on-the-page-describes-the-site)). This doc owns the tokens, the palette and its measured contrast, typography, the motifs, motion, the effects catalog, the design budgets, the accessibility rules and the layout of Home. Why the tokens are a copy owned by this repository is [ADR 0010](adr/0010-quiet-console-design-tokens.md); how the signal box was chosen, over what, is ADR 0025. Instrument, the direction before it (2026-10-23 to 2026-10-30), is kept where a section records its history, and the section says so. Home's sections and copy belong to [content.md](content.md); the policy that admits the theme script belongs to [security.md](security.md); where each check runs belongs to [delivery.md](delivery.md).

## Chosen direction

Chosen on 2026-10-30 ([ADR 0025](adr/0025-signal-box-identity.md)), after an audit of the live site read it as the developer-portfolio template, and Joseph asked for new colours, faces and sizes "to display what I am capable of doing for others". Three directions were drawn on the real content: A "Signal box", B "Space-time" and C "Instrument, evolved". Three judges, through the lenses of craft, feasibility in this codebase and Joseph's own taste, gave A and C the same mean, 7.57 of 10, and B 5.73. The signal box is A's system with C's Home map: the live city with the page as its water.

What the signal box is:

- **One left edge.** Everything stands flush left, ragged right, on one edge. The frame is still 1,312 px of content inside 64 px margins, 18 px on a phone, and from 90rem Home's stage and the Lab index span the column, so a map or a line can reach the edges ([ADR 0024](adr/0024-home-led-by-the-work.md), "A larger screen"). Nothing draws the frame.
- **The name.** Barlow Condensed SemiBold, 56 px at 390 px to 136 px at 1440 px, a ratio of 2.43, on two lines, and at most 20vw. On Home it is the hero; elsewhere the rail or the top bar names Joseph at the navigation's size, and About sets the name at a title's size.
- **Colour is a line.** Each lab draws its line in its own colour (`--line-*`), set by the lab's slug, and colour means which line and nothing else. State is a lamp's shape and a word: filled is done or in service, hollow is not yet, lit warm white (`--primary`) is where the work is now. The primary button is the lit lamp and focus is a 2 px outline in it. Links read as text with a 3:1 underline in `--input`, so the underline, not a colour, marks them (SC 1.4.1).
- **Type is signage.** Barlow Condensed for the name, titles and figures; Barlow Semi Condensed for the navigation, buttons, labels and captions; Barlow for reading; B612 Mono for code alone ([Typography](#typography)). Labels are sentence case, untracked and never mono, and no title has an eyebrow.
- **Square corners, round lamps, no shadows.** `--radius-md` is 0, so buttons, fields, the board and code blocks are square; inline code keeps its 2 px. Lamps are the only round things.
- **A rule works, or it is a line.** A rule is functional, 1 px of `--input` (a field's edge, a button's, the rail's, the top bar's), the board's 2 px bezel, or a lab's line. Rows have no rule between them.
- **Rows react only when they lead somewhere.** A row, a card or a board's band that holds a link sweeps 2 px along its top on hover and on `:focus-within`, in its lab's line, or in the lamp's colour where it has none. One with nothing to activate stays still.
- **No dot and no arrow.** The site writes "Remote, US", "View all demos" and "Up and down to move, Enter to open, Esc to close"; no link or button carries an arrow, and Barlow draws none. `test/tells.test.ts` holds this, the capitals, the tracking and the mono in the build ([Budgets as CI fitness functions](#budgets-as-ci-fitness-functions)).

**Icons.** Chosen on 2026-10-23 from five options on the design canvas. The favicon is a generic self-balancing scooter seen from the front: two wheels and a T handlebar, the lamp's warm white `#f9f3d1` on the night enamel `#22322a` since 2026-10-30 (apricot on petrol before), legible at 16 px. The 180 px touch icon, which iOS and iMessage show, is a rider on the scooter, which needs about 44 px to read, so tabs keep the front view. Both are drawn for this site, not taken from a maker's logo (Q21 in [product.md](product.md)). `public/favicon.svg` and `apps/web/icons/touch-icon.svg` are the sources; `pnpm --filter web icons` renders `favicon.ico` and `apple-touch-icon.png` from them. The touch icon is a full-bleed square, because iOS rounds the corners itself and fills transparency with black. Its wheel is drawn last, over the rider's leg and the stem, with the tile's colour inside the ring: drawn first, the leg's 3-unit stroke filled most of the wheel, which read at 180 px as a crescent with a dark sliver beside it (QA, 2026-10-25). `test/touch-icon.test.ts` reads the PNG and requires every pixel inside the ring to be the tile's color, with a known answer.

**Forced colors.** A Windows contrast theme replaces the primary button's lamp fill with the user's background but keeps the label's own color, which axe measured at 1.02:1. So under `forced-colors: active` the primary button takes system colors (`ButtonFace`, `ButtonText`). The rules between rows are borders, which forced colors draw in the user's text color. System color keywords are the one kind of color allowed outside `tokens.css`. The theme toggle is hidden there: forced colors replace both themes with the visitor's own, so a press would change nothing, and until 2026-10-25 the toggle was shown and focusable anyway (`e2e/theme.spec.ts`).

### Instrument, 2026-10-23 to 2026-10-30

Chosen on 2026-10-23 (Q17 in [product.md](product.md#decided)). The exploration drew three directions on Home's wireframe, at 1440 px and 390 px, in both themes: A "Drawing Set", B "Field Notes" and C "Instrument". Joseph picked C, and then Petrol + Apricot from four color studies of it; Graphite + Cobalt, Ink + Chartreuse, Paper + Swiss red, and C's first palette of graphite and orange lost. Cards keep the hover he preferred: a 2 px accent bar that sweeps across the card's top edge, rather than corner crop marks; the signal box keeps it, in each lab's line.

What C was, as PR 3 built it and 2026-10-24 left it ([The poster setting](#the-poster-setting), [Nothing on the page describes the site](#nothing-on-the-page-describes-the-site)):

- **A 12-column frame.** The frame is 1,312 px of content inside 64 px margins: `max-width: 90rem` on a border-box element, so the padding counts inside it. That sits inside the 1,200 to 1,320 px most sites hold their content to (GitHub's 1,280 px, Tailwind's `max-w-7xl` 1,280 px, Bootstrap's widest container 1,320 px), and a wider screen gets a wider margin. From 2026-10-26 it stopped at 1,792 px for a day, with the display sizes grown to fill it, after Joseph read the page on a 2,240 px screen as "very narrow"; the same day he read that as too wide, and the frame went back. Phones get 18 px margins. Rows sit on twelve columns, four on a phone; the gutter is `--gutter` on `.frame`, 16 px and 28 px from 64rem. The columns are structure, not a picture: nothing draws them. The numbered ruler that did, across the top of every page, left on 2026-10-24 ([The poster setting](#the-poster-setting)).
- **A poster-size name.** 64 px at 390 px and below, 160 px at 1440 px, a ratio of 2.5 (144 px until [ADR 0024](adr/0024-home-led-by-the-work.md)). It is set at 80% width on two lines, both starting at the frame's edge. At 64 px "Peterson" needs about 200 px of the 284 px a 320 px screen leaves. The size also stops at 20vw, which never binds at the default text size from 320 px up; with a larger default text size on a phone it keeps "Peterson" whole on the screen ([Type scale](#type-scale)). Beside the name from 75rem, under it before that: the title, the location and the actions.
- **One accent, as punctuation** (since 2026-10-30, "Colour is a line"). Apricot fills the primary button; it draws focus and the card bar, and colors a link on hover. Otherwise links read as text with an underline at 3:1, so the underline, not the color, marks them (SC 1.4.1). `--signal` and `--ring` point at the accent.
- **Type.** Instrument Sans, whose width axis runs from 75% to 100% and gives the condensed name, card titles and page titles, and JetBrains Mono for a post's dates and kind, a card's tag and figures, and code; both OFL 1.1, self-hosted ([Typography](#typography)).
- **Square corners.** `--radius-md` is 0: buttons and code blocks are square, as on a printed sheet. Inline code keeps its 2 px.
- **Cards react only when they lead somewhere.** A card is a row of an index under a rule. A card that holds a link shows the bar over its rule and a stronger rule on hover, and `:focus-within` mirrors it for the keyboard. A card with nothing to activate stays still, because a reaction would promise a click that does nothing. Since 2026-10-24 Home draws no such card: a card renders only with a page to open (`lib/work.ts`), and the stylesheet keeps the rule, which `e2e/motion.spec.ts` checks on a card it adds.

### The poster setting

A record of Instrument: since 2026-10-30 the name is 56→136 px in Barlow Condensed, and Home's layout is the signal box's ([Home, row by row](#home-row-by-row)). Its rule stands: what shows how the page is built goes.

Set on 2026-10-24, from Joseph's ask to make the site exceptional and take a few risks, then pared back the same day by his direction that the site stop demonstrating ([next section](#nothing-on-the-page-describes-the-site)). Home reads as a printed poster: the same tokens, faces, rows and checks, with the name set large.

- **The name is the poster.** Two lines at 64→144 px in the condensed face. The `<h1>` holds the two words and nothing else, so its accessible name, its link preview and the smoke check are unchanged. The break comes from the box being as wide as its widest line (`min-content`). The box starts 0.055em left of the frame, the side bearing of "P" in both the web face and the fitted fallback, so the stem of "P" lines up with the frame's edge.
- **Rules, not boxes.** Section titles and the footer hang from rules; cards and posts in a list are rows of an index under a 3:1 rule. What separates things on Home is a rule or a column.
- **Columns as structure.** From 75rem the hero's facts stand in columns 9 to 12 (from 64rem until 2026-10-25, but below about 70rem the title needs two lines there); a card or a post in a list puts its tag or date in columns 1 and 2, its title in 3 to 8 and its small print in 9 to 12; a long-form page's head puts its label in columns 1 and 2 and the rest from column 3. On a phone each stacks.
- **What left on 2026-10-24, and why.** Each of these showed how the page was built, or decorated it, and none told a visitor anything about Joseph:
  - the ruler, a numbered tick band over twelve columns on every page and on the link preview image: the layout grid on display;
  - the step, "Joseph" indented by one column with an apricot field in the step: a stranger read the field as a missing photo or logo;
  - section numbers and How I work's numerals: numbering for its own sake, which stacked two "01"s when one section rendered;
  - How I work, four statements on a full-width apricot field: the site's own build rules restated as principles, with no evidence, given the most weight on the page;
  - the square location mark, the `hello@` hint on the Email button, the footer's "Contact" label and its address set at 24→60 px, the "Error 404" label, and the toggle's words ("Theme: system", and "saved in this browser" after a press);
  - the domain as the header's wordmark: the header names Joseph instead, on every page but Home, where the `<h1>` does.
- **What it cost.** Nothing added: the theme script lost 243 B (789 → 546 B gzip -9) and CSS went from 6,686 to 5,916 B. The fold moved from 459.1 to 368.2 px of 844 on a phone and from 541.0 to 420.7 px of 900 on a laptop ([Budgets](#budgets-as-ci-fitness-functions)).
- **What would show it was wrong.** A recruiter shown Home for 7 seconds who cannot recall the name, or reviewers calling Home unfinished while it holds the hero alone. The second is a content gap, not a layout one: Selected work and Writing render as soon as a post is published or a project's checks pass.

### Nothing on the page describes the site

Set on 2026-10-24, from Joseph's direction: "There is a difference between demonstrating and executing." The site is for a visitor who wants to know who Joseph is, see his work, read his writing and reach him. So nothing on any page exists to show how the site is built.

- **What left.** Home's readout row (Build, Built, JavaScript), the footer's build line on every other page, and the "This site" card with its JavaScript and fonts figures. No page prints a commit, a build date or a figure about the site's own bytes, and none ever draws a gauge or a meter. The served-from readout planned for PR 4 is dropped with them. A second pass the same day took out the decoration that showed the grid or the build ([The poster setting](#the-poster-setting)), the comments in the inline theme script and in `robots.txt`, which every visitor's browser downloads, and the build's commit from every page's markup.
- **What stays, unprinted.** The byte budgets are enforced in CI exactly as before (`test/budgets.test.ts`, through `src/lib/weights.ts`). Which commit is live is a machine's question: the build adds `X-Build: <full commit>` to the `/*` rule of `dist/_headers` (`src/lib/build.mjs`, through `astro.config.mjs`), so every response carries it as a header. The deploy smoke reads it (`e2e/smoke.spec.ts`); no page holds it.
- **Checked.** `test/readouts.test.ts` requires `_headers` to send the commit the checkout builds, once, under `/*`, finds the commit in no page and in no other file the build writes, and holds Home and the 404 to no byte figure, no build readout and no "This site" card, with known answers. `e2e/fold.spec.ts` expects the hero alone above the fold.
- **The rule for what comes next.** A new element earns its place by telling the visitor something about Joseph or his work. One that tells them about the site is left out, however true it is.
- **Joseph's own words may name the site as his work.** Since 2026-10-29 Home opens with the bio's first sentence, "This site is that work, in the open", and its principles cite the site's decisions as evidence of how he works; he cleared both with the bio. So `test/readouts.test.ts` looks for a card titled "This site", not for the words. A drawing of the site's own architecture, which is how one reference site opens, stays out ([ADR 0023](adr/0023-home-as-a-story-and-rail-drop-downs.md)).

## Why

- Recruiters give the page seconds and engineers give it a minute ([content.md](content.md) has the evidence). A slow, hard-to-read or templated page contradicts what the site says about its author, so speed and accessibility are requirements here, not polish.
- The ask was a "cyber" feel. The need is the work. Structure earns its place only where it helps a reader find it: the type carries the name, the work and the writing, and since 2026-10-30 the board draws every system Joseph can show as its line, so a team sees consensus, routing and, as the labs ship, queues, caches and pipelines before it reads a word. The site's own engineering is checked by machines and not shown (principle 6 in [product.md](product.md), as revised on 2026-10-24).
- If nothing is done, the site gets shadcn's stock look, which reads as a template, or a sci-fi skin that delays the first word. Instrument showed a third way to fail: a site of its own that still read as the developer-portfolio template, through its near-black background and its chrome ([ADR 0025](adr/0025-signal-box-identity.md)).

## Tokens (CSS)

`apps/web/src/styles/tokens.css` is the only file in `apps/web/src` that may hold a color value; `test/color-literals.test.ts` fails on a literal anywhere else, system color keywords excepted. This section explains the file rather than copying it.

- **Two tiers.** Tier 1 (primitives) are the `oklch()` literals, and they live only in the theme blocks. Tier 2 (semantic) uses shadcn's names, and components use only these, through Tailwind (`bg-background`, `text-muted-foreground`) or `var()`. Tier 3 (component) tokens narrow a semantic token for one component. Since 2026-10-30 there is one, `--line`: a lab's slug sets it to that lab's line colour (`[data-line='raft'] { --line: var(--line-raft) }`, in `global.css`), so a drawing, a row's sweep or a lab page's head says `var(--line)` and never names a lab. Home's scene narrows the van tokens the same way, in its own scope, so every van there is the Route Lab's line. The weaker design gives each component a lab's token by name, and a new lab then edits every component that draws one. The weaker design lets a component carry its own literal: then a theme change becomes a search across every file, and the contrast test cannot see the value.
- **Tailwind's own scales are removed** (`--color-*`, `--text-*`, `--radius-*` and `--font-*` set to `initial`), so a stray utility class produces nothing instead of an untested color. `@theme inline` maps the semantic tokens to Tailwind's names.
- **Themes.** Dark is the designed default: `:root` and `:root[data-theme='dark']` share one block. A stored light choice has its own block, and the OS's light preference has a second copy under `prefers-color-scheme: light`, scoped with `:where()` so a stored dark choice still wins. The test fails if the two light copies differ.
- **More contrast.** Under `prefers-contrast: more`, `subtle-foreground` steps up to `muted-foreground` and `border` to `input`, over any stored theme.
- **Print.** Paper is white whatever the screen showed: the print block sets the surfaces to white, text to black and the lamp to ink, the labs' lines to their day colours, and the maps to theirs, with the land in its own gray so it still parts from the white water. Since 2026-10-25 it sets the hairlines too, to a neutral gray a printer keeps (2.2:1 on white), and underlines and fields to 4.9:1; before, they kept the screen theme's, near-white from light and dark gray from dark. The primary button prints as an outline with ink text, so it reads the same whether or not the browser prints backgrounds. Paper gets no control: the theme toggle, the skip link and the nav are not printed, nor is Home's header, which holds nothing else. A link whose words are not its address prints the address after it in small label type, the site's own with the domain, so a post's citations survive on paper; in running text the address flows with the words. A button prints no address: the footer prints the same ones. Inline code drops its padding on paper and in forced colors, where it read as a stray space. `e2e/print.spec.ts` checks every page on the site and on the writing fixture. The résumé has print rules of its own, on one named page ([The résumé](#the-résumé)).
- **Specificity.** The more-contrast and print blocks name `:root[data-theme]` too, so they outrank every theme block, stored or not. A bare `:root` would lose to `[data-theme]`, and a stored dark theme would print near-white text.

One alternative was considered and deferred: `light-dark()` with `color-scheme: light dark` would follow the OS with no copy of the light values, and would define each token once. It reaches Baseline widely available around 2026-11 (newly available on 2024-05-13), and a browser without it would get no colors at all, while the stored choice still needs the pre-paint script. Revisit when the token file is next restructured.

Browser support, from the [web-features](https://github.com/web-platform-dx/web-features) Baseline data as of 2026-10-22: `oklch()` and `color-mix()` are Baseline widely available (since 2025-11-09); cross-document view transitions (Chrome 126, Safari 18.2) are not Baseline because Firefox lacks them, which is why they sit behind a working default; `:has()`, which the card's hover uses, is newly available, and the keyboard state does not depend on it.

## Theme before first paint

The theme must be set before the first paint, or a visitor whose stored choice differs from the OS sees one frame in the wrong theme. `src/scripts/theme.js` does it, inlined in `<head>`:

- **What it sets.** With `dark` or `light` stored for this tab, `data-theme` takes that value. Otherwise `data-theme="system"`, and CSS treats `system` exactly like a missing attribute: both follow the OS.
- **The back-forward cache.** A page the browser restores from its back-forward cache comes back as the visitor left it, and the script does not run again, so a choice made on another page in between would not show: QA saw the old theme on a restored page while `sessionStorage` held the new one (2026-10-25). The script listens for `pageshow` and, when `event.persisted` says the page came from the cache, applies the stored choice again. When storage refuses to be read, the restored page keeps the choice it shows, since a choice storage refused holds for the page it was made on.
- **The fail-safe is the OS.** No stored choice, storage that throws (private modes, blocked storage), a blocked script and no JavaScript at all share one path: the page follows `prefers-color-scheme`. Storage access sits in `try`/`catch`.
- **Where it runs.** In `<head>`, right after the `color-scheme` and `theme-color` metas and before any stylesheet, so it has always run before the first frame that can paint `<body>`, however the HTML arrives. PR 3 first placed it as the first child of `<body>`, and the first Worker Preview showed the gap: when the HTML arrived in pieces, a frame painted before the script ran.
- **It is governed.** The page policy is a header the build writes, with a hash of each inline script a page holds, this one among them, so it applies before the first byte of markup ([security.md](security.md#headers-and-content-security-policy), [ADR 0018](adr/0018-page-policy-as-a-header.md)). An edit cannot leave a stale hash behind: the build hashes what the page holds. `test/csp.test.ts` requires the script to be the first, in `<head>`, byte for byte the file, and an e2e test places a copy with one byte changed first in `<head>` and requires the browser to refuse it. Until 2026-10-25 the policy was Astro's `<meta>` at the end of `<head>`, which covers only what follows it, so this one script ran ungoverned, an exception those two checks pinned.
- **The browser chrome follows the page.** Two `<meta name="theme-color">` tags, one per OS scheme (`media="(prefers-color-scheme: dark)"` and `light`), carry the `background` token of their theme as `#rrggbb`, resolved from `tokens.css` at build (`chromeColor` in `src/lib/tokens.ts`), so `src/` holds no color literal and a palette change moves the bar above the page with it. Without JavaScript they follow the OS, as the page does. A stored theme would leave the bar on the OS's color, so the script, at load and on every press, gives both metas the stored theme's color, and System gives each its own back. They come before the script, which finds them before the first paint. `test/theme-color.test.ts` pins both values to the token on every page of every build, with their order; `e2e/theme.spec.ts` requires the color a browser would take from them to be the body's painted background in every case below.
- **`theme` and `motion` are the only keys the site stores, and only for the tab** (`sessionStorage`, [security.md](security.md#privacy-and-data-inventory)); `motion` holds a pause of Home's moving parts, from 2026-10-29: the living ground at first ([ADR 0021](adr/0021-rail-and-living-ground.md)), since [ADR 0024](adr/0024-home-led-by-the-work.md) the Route Lab's scene and the Raft Lab's election, and since [ADR 0025](adr/0025-signal-box-identity.md) the scene and the board's day. A choice holds across the pages of a visit and a reload; a new tab starts from the OS again. Until 2026-10-24 the choice went to `localStorage`, which kept it across visits but needed the toggle to say "saved in this browser", the site explaining its own storage on every press. An e2e test reads both stores after the toggle has run.
- **The toggle** is an icon button at the end of the header, and the same script handles its clicks, so it adds no script of its own. It cycles System, then the theme the OS is not showing, then the one it is, and back to System: on a dark OS, System, Light, Dark. Every press but the return to System changes the page, so a first press never looks broken. Each state has its own icon, which CSS picks from `data-theme`: a half-filled circle for System, a sun for Light, a moon for Dark. So every press changes what the toggle shows, the return to System included; until 2026-10-25 one icon served all three, and on a dark OS the press from Dark to System changed nothing visible. Its name, "Theme: System", "Theme: Day" or "Theme: Night" since 2026-10-30 (the panel's words; "Theme: system", "light" and "dark" before), is in the one of three state spans CSS leaves displayed, and the span carries a `title` with the same words over the whole button, for a pointer. In the top bar the name is clipped, so only assistive technology reads it; at the rail's foot the icon and its words show, as "Pause motion" does beside it. It is 44 × 44 px in the top bar. Without JavaScript nothing sets `data-theme`, so the toggle stays hidden, and print and forced colors hide it too: there is never a control that does nothing. If storage refuses a choice, it holds for the page.
- **No comments ship.** The script is inlined as written, byte for byte the file the CSP hash is computed from, so what it does is told here and in `Base.astro`, not in the file.
- **Size.** The script measured 624 B gzip on 2026-10-23, against the 0.3 KB the plan estimated for the toggle alone. Keeping the browser chrome in step added 165 B: 789 B gzip (1,669 B as written) on 2026-10-24. Without its comments and the saved flag, the same day: 546 B gzip (1,185 B as written). Applying the choice again when the back-forward cache restores a page, and keeping the page's own choice there when storage refuses, added 45 B: 591 B gzip (1,356 B as written) on 2026-10-25. It was the only JavaScript on a content page until the command menu's module joined it on every page, the same day ([The command menu](#the-command-menu)).
- **Tested** (`e2e/theme.spec.ts`): the first frame that can paint `<body>` already has the right `data-theme`, background and browser-chrome color in eight cases, a dark or light OS with nothing, light, dark or throwing storage chosen; the cycle on both OSes, where every press but the last changes the page, the chrome follows each press, `theme` is the only key in `sessionStorage` and `localStorage` stays empty; a choice survives a reload, a new tab starts from the OS, and refused storage still applies the choice to the page; System follows an OS change live, chrome included; a chosen theme of either kind prints black on white, the Email button included, with the toggle hidden; more contrast applies over a chosen theme; and without JavaScript the page and its chrome follow the OS and show no toggle. ## Typography

### The faces

Since 2026-10-30 ([ADR 0025](adr/0025-signal-box-identity.md)) the site sets type as signage, in one design, Barlow, in three widths, and sets code in B612 Mono. Each file is a static instance from google/fonts at commit `0b58fb37`, cut to Google Fonts' "latin" range (Latin-1 and the general punctuation block) as unhinted WOFF2.

| Face | Weight | Job | Bytes | Preloaded | Source | Licence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Barlow Condensed SemiBold | 600 | the name, page and section titles, a row's title, the figures, How I work's leads | 12,728 | yes | Barlow Condensed 1.408, Jeremy Tribby | OFL 1.1, no Reserved Font Name |
| Barlow Semi Condensed Medium | 500 | the navigation, buttons, labels, status lines, describers, dates, a key's name, captions, the labels inside drawings | 13,632 | yes | Barlow Semi Condensed 1.408 | the same |
| Barlow Regular | 400 | reading text: prose, leads, summaries, a card's text | 13,428 | yes | Barlow 1.408 | the same |
| Barlow SemiBold | 600 | `<strong>` in reading text | 13,560 | no: fetched where a page draws it | Barlow 1.408 | the same |
| B612 Mono Regular | 400 | code, and nothing else | 12,084 | no: declared only on a page with code | B612 Mono 1.008, Intactile DESIGN for Airbus | OFL 1.1, no Reserved Font Name |

The three preloads come to 39,788 B against the 70 KB budget, where Instrument Sans alone took 55,984 B. Every page declares the four Barlow files, 53,348 B, and a page with code adds B612 Mono's 12,084 B (`Base.astro`'s `code`, which `Prose.astro` sets after rendering the body). So the Route Lab's first view declares 53,348 B of fonts, against 64,140 B with Instrument Sans and JetBrains Mono. `e2e/glyphs.spec.ts` requires every font file a page fetches to be a face that draws some of its text, matching each fetched file to the face Chromium reports by the file's own family name, with a known answer. `public/fonts/` carries both licence files beside the fonts.

Why three widths and not the two families the brief allowed: Barlow's widths are one design, one designer's, with one x-height, one stroke and one licence, so they read as one voice in three registers, a nameplate, an engraved label and the instructions, where two unrelated families would sit further apart. What the two-family rule protected is the preload budget, and the three files fit it with 30 KB to spare. Barlow takes after California's public signage, its licence plates, highway signs, buses and trains; B612 was drawn for Airbus's cockpit screens, for legibility at a glance.

- **Cut from pinned sources.** `apps/web/scripts/fonts/build.sh` downloads each source into a fresh temporary directory, refuses any file whose size or sha256 differs from `fonts.lock.json` before a font parser reads it, and cuts with fontTools from hash-pinned wheels in a throwaway environment (`cut.py`). `build.sh --write` records the outputs, and a second `build.sh` proves the cut reproduces them byte for byte. CI never runs it: `test/fonts.test.ts` checks every committed and served font against the lock, byte for byte, and checks each file's name strings and tables, with known answers.
- **Layout features.** Kerning, the fi and fl ligatures, composition and local forms, and both figure widths, since Barlow's figures are proportional and changing numbers set tabular ones. `calt` and the fractions are dropped, so no face draws `=>` as an arrow in code.
- **Delivery.** Astro's Fonts API with the local provider serves the files from `/_astro/fonts/`, writes a preload link for each face that asks for one, and emits a `<style>` per family, which the build hashes into the page policy's `style-src` ([ADR 0018](adr/0018-page-policy-as-a-header.md)). Its own fallbacks are off: the fitted faces below replace them.
- **Fitted fallback faces.** `src/styles/font-fallbacks.css`, written by `build.sh`, holds one fallback family per web face and local font: Arial (with Liberation Sans and Arimo, which share its widths), Roboto for Android and DejaVu Sans for Linux without Liberation; for B612 Mono, Menlo or DejaVu Sans Mono, and Courier New, Liberation Mono or Cousine. Each has `size-adjust` and ascent, descent and line-gap overrides fitted to its web face: Barlow Condensed's to "Joseph Peterson", read from `profile.json`, since the name is Home's largest paint; the others to English letter frequencies. Text breaks in the same place before and after the web font arrives, and the fold check measures the hero both ways.
- **Link previews.** Satori reads TrueType, not WOFF2, so `build.sh` also cuts the two condensed widths as static `.ttf` files (`src/og/fonts/`): the card's title in Barlow Condensed and its label in Barlow Semi Condensed. They never reach the browser.
- **No arrow and no ⌘.** Barlow draws neither, so links and buttons say where they go in words, a footnote's way back is the word "Back", and the command menu's ⌘ is drawn as an inline SVG beside its "K", hidden from assistive technology ([The command menu](#the-command-menu)).

Self-hosting is not only about bytes. Since Chrome 86 the HTTP cache is partitioned per site ([Chrome](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/http-cache-partitioning)), so a shared Google Fonts cache gives no benefit. A third-party font also needs its own connection setup ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/font-best-practices)), and every request to it hands the visitor's IP address to another company.

Until 2026-10-30 the faces were Instrument Sans, a variable font whose width axis gave the condensed name at 80%, and a JetBrains Mono subset for dates, a card's tag, figures and code: 55,984 and 8,156 B ([ADR 0010](adr/0010-quiet-console-design-tokens.md)).

### Type scale

Eight sizes since 2026-10-30, from about 15 before. Each runs linearly from 390 px to 1440 px and stops at both ends, so a wider screen gets more picture, not bigger letters; each clamp is computed exactly from its two sizes (`tokens.css`).

| Token | 390 → 1440 px | Line height | Face | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| title | 42 → 72, and at most 13.125vw | 0.95 | Barlow Condensed 600 | a page's `<h1>`, a featured lab's title, the figures, the closing address (at the section's size below 27rem, where it would not fit a phone's column) |
| section | 30 → 48 | 1.0 | Barlow Condensed 600 | a section's `<h2>` |
| headline | 24 → 28 | 1.1 | Barlow Condensed 600 | an index row's title, a post's `<h2>`, How I work's leads (a step under their section's title, since 2026-10-30: at the title's size the title read as one more lead), a board line's title at room size |
| lead | 19 → 22 | 1.4 | Barlow 400 | a page's lead, the 404's text |

The retired sizes, each mapped to its role: kv, label, meta and sm to label; card to base; h3 and h2 to headline; statement to section, or to title for a page's title; poster and figure to title. Display type may tighten a little (−0.005em to −0.012em); nothing else is tracked.

Rules:

- **Mono for code.** B612 Mono sets code and nothing else: no value, date, figure or label is mono, so a page without code declares no mono face. Until 2026-10-30 the rule was "mono for values, never prose".
- **Labels are signage.** Sentence case, untracked, never mono, and no eyebrow over any title. A key's name is in the label face too: Search's "Ctrl K" and the command menu's keys. Since 2026-10-30 `test/tells.test.ts` fails the build on `text-transform: uppercase`, on any letter-spacing above zero, on a rule that sets the label face with letter-spacing other than 0, and on a monospaced face set on anything but code, in `src/styles` and in the build's own stylesheets, utilities included, with known answers copied from main before the change.
- Fluid sizes mix `rem` and `vw`, each has a `rem` floor, and each maximum is at most 2.5 times its minimum (the hero: 2.43), so 200% zoom still enlarges text (SC 1.4.4; Maxwell Barvian, ["Addressing Accessibility Concerns With Using Fluid Type"](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2023/11/addressing-accessibility-concerns-fluid-type/), 2023-11-07). `test/tokens.test.ts` checks all three.
- **A cap in vw, only where it never binds at the default size.** A fluid size may also stop at a width, `min(<clamp>, <n>vw)`, when n vw at 320 px is at least what the clamp gives there and n is at least the clamp's own vw rate: at the default text size the cap then never binds from 320 px up, and zoom enlarges the text exactly as without it. The hero has one, 20vw, set on 2026-10-25 after QA found the name broken as "Peterso / n" with the browser's text at 24 px and 32 px on a phone; at 320 px it is 64 px, above the hero's 56 px floor. The title has another since 2026-10-30, 13.125vw, exactly its 42 px floor at 320 px: at twice the default size "deliveries" ran 2 px past a 320 px screen without it. A cap binds only with a larger default text size on a narrow screen, where the words could not grow without breaking inside a word or scrolling sideways. `test/tokens.test.ts` checks both caps, and still refuses PR 2's 16.5vw cap, which bound below 400 px at the default size.
- Numbers that change or line up (a clock, dates, figures in a table) use tabular numerals.
- Titles balance their lines (`text-wrap: balance`); paragraphs avoid a short last line (`pretty`).
- **The measure.** Body text runs about 68 characters a line: `--measure: 31em` in `tokens.css`. Not `68ch`: `ch` is the width of the digit zero, which differs from face to face (Instrument Sans drew it wide, and a 68ch column measured 95 to 103 characters a line of English at 16 px, past the 80 that SC 1.4.8 asks for). At 31em the paragraph ran 62 to 71 in Instrument Sans (2026-10-23), and `e2e/writing.spec.ts` counts the characters a line in the face a reader gets, Barlow since 2026-10-30.

## Motifs

The feel comes from the work, drawn as lines. Each motif is CSS, SVG drawn at build or real text, and each one survives with JavaScript off.

- **The name.** Two lines in Barlow Condensed, at the hero's size on Home ([Type scale](#type-scale)).
- **The lines.** Each live lab drawn as the real shape of its system, in its line's colour: the Route Lab as the bakery's depot with a track per van, each stop a lamp placed by when the van reaches it; the Raft Lab as a hub and its spokes, the leader to each follower, then its log as a track of lamps. Home's board draws them on one panel, the Lab index draws them large beside each lab's words, and each lab's page runs its line across its head into a filled lamp. `lib/lines.ts` works every line out of the lab's own run at build, and a new lab adds its line as one component in `components/board/lines/`.
- **Lamps.** The only round things. Filled is done or in service, hollow is not yet, lit warm white is where the work is now; a pressed or chosen button shows a lit 10 px lamp before its words instead of a colour fill, and the command menu's selected row a lit one. A word always says the same as the lamp.
- **The board and the desk.** The recessed surface (`--surface-1`) in a 2 px bezel of `--input`: Home's board holds the lines, a lab's desk holds its demo, and the command menu sits in one too.
- **The sweep.** The 2 px bar that runs along a row's top when the row leads somewhere, in its lab's line, or in the lamp's colour where it has none ([Chosen direction](#chosen-direction)).
- **Timestamps.** Dates in words, "25 October 2026", with the ISO 8601 day in each `<time>`'s `datetime`, and no time of day and no zone: midnight UTC carries neither, and anything else would tell a reader when or where the build ran. A post's dates are the only dates a page shows. `test/readouts.test.ts` scans every served file for a time of day or an offset in every common form. No ticking clock: a clock is auto-updating content under SC 2.2.2, and it says nothing about the work.
- **Removed: coordinates and a fixed UTC offset.** The research wireframe put one-decimal coordinates and a UTC offset in the hero label. Both are gone. One decimal of latitude and longitude resolves to about 11 km, which is city scale; next to a title, years and an industry, that narrows the search to one employer, which is the mosaic effect the content firewall exists to prevent ([ADR 0007](adr/0007-content-firewall.md)). A fixed offset is also wrong for half the year: a US offset moves by one hour at each daylight saving change (the next one is 2026-11-01). The hero carries only the location line Joseph chose (Q5 · Q-location in [product.md](product.md)).
- **Page geometry (2026-10-27).** About's wheels, behind the page's top right, and the 404's line, which ends in a buffer stop under its words ([Page geometry](#page-geometry)).
- **Removed on 2026-10-30** ([ADR 0025](adr/0025-signal-box-identity.md)): the eyebrows over titles, capitals and tracking on labels, mono for values, the middle dots and the arrows, How I work's numbers, the isometric pictures, Writing's ruled sheet and the Lab's street plans behind their pages, and rules between rows.
- **Removed on 2026-10-24:** the ruler, the step and its field, section numbers, the location mark and How I work's field ([The poster setting](#the-poster-setting)).
- **Removed with Quiet Console:** the hairline grid, corner brackets, status LEDs, scanlines and the hero's terminal panel ([Lineage](#lineage)).

**How it is made.** `src/lib/ground.ts` generates each drawing at build from the section and the page's path, through a seeded generator, so the same page always draws the same lines and its markup changes only when the drawing does. `components/Ground.astro` writes it as an inline SVG in `<body>`, before the masthead: hidden from assistive technology, no pointer events, classes and never style attributes (the page policy refuses those), and hidden in print. It sits at its natural size behind the top right from 75rem, cut by the window below that, and two masks fade it toward the text column and toward the fold. Nothing moves.

**Home's drawing came alive, then gave way (2026-10-29).** From [ADR 0021](adr/0021-rail-and-living-ground.md) a WebGL2 shader drew Home's contours again on the GPU, turning from terrain to a circuit board to cells. [ADR 0024](adr/0024-home-led-by-the-work.md) removed it and Home's drawing with it: Home's picture is now the Route Lab's day, driven on the map behind the hero ([Motion](#motion)), so Home carries no ground of its own.

**Why generated and not traced from the map.** The Route Lab's isochrones and streets come from OpenStreetMap. Drawn on every page, they would be a produced work under the ODbL, and every page would need its notice. They would also cost about 30 KB gzip of map data where the generated drawing costs about 2 KB. A generated drawing has neither cost and looks the same at this weight.

**Contrast.** Every line of the wheels is `--ground`, a token the contrast test treats as a surface, so every text token reads on it at 4.5:1 and a word that crosses a line keeps its contrast ([Measured contrast](#measured-contrast)). The one mark, the buffer stop's lit lamp, is `--primary`, which is not a surface, so `e2e/ground.spec.ts` requires every mark to touch no text on every kind of page at 320, 390, 768 and 1440 px, with a known answer that moves a mark onto the name; and it holds the 404's line to the column's right edge and clear of the button at 390, 1440 and 2560 px.

**Weight.** At most 2,500 B gzip of path data per drawing, in `test/ground.test.ts`. Home's contours were the heaviest, at 2,318 B, until Home's drawing left with ADR 0024. Every coordinate is a whole number.

**Weaker designs.** A CSS background image would be cached, but it cannot read the page's color tokens, so it would need one file per theme and a mask to recolor it. A canvas or WebGL drawing would cost JavaScript and battery on every visit for a picture that never changes. Real map data would bring the license notice and the bytes. A drawing under the whole page would sit under body text, where the faint lines would compete with reading.

- **Author motion inside `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`** (no-motion-first). State changes live outside the query, so a reduced-motion visitor still sees a row's bar and the board's dimming; only the movement is removed. `test/tokens.test.ts` fails if a transition, an animation, smooth scrolling or a view transition appears anywhere else, and knows the stylesheets that move nothing: the Raft Lab's, the lines' and the Lab index's.
- **Animate `transform` and `opacity`.** Never animate layout properties such as width, height, margin or top. The board's day animates a registered integer, `--day`, which nothing but `opacity` reads.
- **Nothing moves for more than 5 s without a pause control** (SC 2.2.2): the header's button pauses Home's scene and the board's day together. **Nothing flashes more than 3 times a second** (SC 2.3.1): a lamp changes at most once a second.
- **No motion on body copy, and never on the LCP element.** The hero name is the likely LCP element; it is never faded or delayed.
- **Text never animates** (2026-10-30). No title rises, no rule draws itself, no label decodes and no arrow nudges; `e2e/motion.spec.ts` requires a row's words to stay the same under the pointer and the keyboard, and finds the sweeps and the board's day moving and nothing else.
- **Nothing a pointer moves may shift the layout.** A pointer's movement is not an input that excuses a shift, so a shift it causes counts against CLS in the field, where no lab test hovers. That is why the hero name does not tune its width to the pointer ([Deferred](#deferred)).
- **One moving thing on screen at a time. No bounce or elastic easing.** Home's scene is one thing, its caption included, and the board runs the same day: it moves only while the scene runs, one button pauses both, and the scene has left with the Route Lab's chapter before the board comes up. The lift into the cube moves only while the visitor scrolls, and runs back when they scroll up, so it is the scroll that moves, not the page.
- **Never fade text in.** A title half way through a fade is half transparent, under 4.5:1, and axe reads it that way.
- **Keyboard-driven UI opens instantly, and frequent actions animate less** (Rauno Freiberg, see [Prior art](#prior-art)). The command menu has no open animation: it is there on the press.

## Long-form pages (PR 5)

Posts now, and the colophon with its decision records in PR 3b, share one layout: `layouts/Prose.astro`, with `components/PageHead.astro` for the head and `components/PostList.astro` for the lists on Home, `/writing/` and `/notes/`. The layout knows nothing about posts: a page passes in its words and puts its body in the slot.

- **Head, on the page's one left edge** (since 2026-10-30). No eyebrow: the page's one `<h1>` in Barlow Condensed at the title's size, then the summary as the lead. A post has a register beside it from 64rem, in the label face and muted: its kind ("Draft essay" on a preview's draft page), its day in words and its tags as plain words; on a phone the register is a line under the lead. An index's head is its title and its lead. The 404 takes the same head. Until 2026-10-30 a mono label stood over the title (the kind, or an index's post count) and a mono line of dates and tags under it. `test/headings.test.ts` holds every page to one `<h1>`, read from the rendered HTML, so no way of writing a heading in a body gets around it.
- **Body.** Markdown as written, in Barlow at the base size and the measure. Tailwind's preflight removes every margin, list marker and heading size, so `.prose` sets them once: an h2 at the headline size, list markers, a quotation's 3:1 rule, links underlined in `--input` that turn to the ink on hover, and code in B612 Mono on `surface-1`, the only mono on the site.
- **Code wraps.** A code block wraps its lines rather than scrolling. A scrolling block would have to take keyboard focus (axe's `scrollable-region-focusable`), and at 320 px it would scroll sideways (SC 1.4.10). There is no syntax highlighting yet: Shiki writes inline styles the policy would refuse, and Expressive Code arrives with the first post that needs it ([stack.md](stack.md)).
- **Tables.** They fill the column at the small size, with tabular figures and hairline rules, and wrap their cells, so a table never scrolls sideways at 320 px. Column alignment is not supported: Markdown writes it as a `style` attribute, which the policy refuses, and `test/csp.test.ts` names it.
- **Footnotes.** As the processor writes them: a section after the body at the small size, above a hairline, and its heading for assistive technology only (`.sr-only`, the same rule as Tailwind's utility, which the build cannot see in the processor's output). The way back is the word "Back" since 2026-10-30, written by a callback for every reference so that a second reference's way back reads "Back" inside its link's name ("Back to reference 1-2"; SC 2.5.3); Barlow draws neither "↑", which it was until then, nor the processor's "↩" (`astro.config.mjs`).
- **Lists.** A post is a row: its title as a link in Barlow Condensed at the headline size, its summary, then its day in words and its kind after it in the label face ("25 October 2026, postmortem"), with no rule between rows; the whole row is its title's link, and the sweep runs along its top on hover and focus ([ADR 0022](adr/0022-rail-as-index-and-row-links.md)). Newest first; a day's posts by slug, so the order never depends on the disk. Until 2026-10-30 the date and kind stood in mono in columns 1 and 2, under a 3:1 rule.
- **Not yet.** Images in posts. `test/dist-files.test.ts` admits PNG, ICO and WOFF2 binaries only, Astro writes a post's images as WebP, and its asset pipeline emits a draft's images in a production build, so images need a WebP check, an image budget and drafts kept out of the asset pipeline first. Until then `test/writing.test.ts` refuses a Markdown image in a post's source, with that reason, and `test/prose.test.ts` an `img`, `picture` or `source` inside a post's rendered `div.prose`, on every build a Vitest project reads. Raw HTML is not refused yet, and can show an image another way.

## The command menu

Shipped on 2026-10-25, from PR 4's plan: `components/Palette.astro` writes it into every page, and `src/scripts/palette.js`, one module with no imports, opens it. ⌘K or Ctrl+K opens and closes it, never a bare key; so do its button in the top bar and the rail's Search field (in the footer until 2026-10-29), which show under a pointer that can hover, once the script has bound the menu (until then they hold their place unseen, so nothing moves), and never without JavaScript or on a touch screen, where the nav, the toggle and the footer hold all it offers. They name the keys this keyboard has, "Ctrl K", or on an Apple keyboard the command key and "K", in the label face; Barlow has no ⌘, so since 2026-10-30 it is drawn as an inline SVG, hidden from assistive technology; the letters stay in the buttons' names, "Search Ctrl K" and "Command menu Ctrl K", since in the bar they are the whole visible label (SC 2.5.3), and `aria-keyshortcuts` tells the keys as well. Until then the keys were in mono, the face that had ⌘.

- **What it is.** A native `<dialog>` opened with `showModal()`, so the page behind it is inert, Esc closes it, and focus returns to whatever opened it. In it, a labelled combobox, "Find a page or an action", and a listbox; `aria-activedescendant` follows the selected row, which `aria-selected` marks, alone. Since 2026-10-30 it is the board's recessed surface in a 2 px bezel, and the selected row is raised (`--surface-2`) with its lamp lit, the one focus indication there: the input keeps its own ring only while it has focus, where the audit found two rings at once. It opens in 0 ms, with no animation, near the top of the screen, where an on-screen keyboard does not cover it.
- **Its rows.** Grouped as Go to, Lab, Writing, Actions and Links. Go to is Home and the manifest's pages this build writes ([One nav manifest](#one-nav-manifest)); Lab and Writing, since 2026-10-29, are every demo and every published post, so the rail's search field finds what the rail does not list ([ADR 0022](adr/0022-rail-as-index-and-row-links.md)); Actions copy the email address and press the theme toggle, whose names the entry shows; Links are the profiles whose readiness check is recorded. Every row exists elsewhere on the page too, so the menu is a shortcut, never the only way in ([content.md](content.md#sitemap-and-phases)); copying the address is the one action it adds.
- **Finding a row.** Typing narrows the rows by subsequence over each label, the value beside it and each keyword, each on its own, without case or accents, so "rsm" and "cv" both find Résumé. A demo's or a post's title is matched word by word or whole, and its summary only whole, since across a dozen words a subsequence finds almost anything. A group with no row left is hidden, and the first row that holds the query whole is selected, else the first match, so Enter acts at once and "bak" opens the bakery, not "Break a Raft cluster". ↑ and ↓ move the selection and wrap; Enter or a click acts, and with ⌘ or Ctrl held a page opens in a new tab. A click on the backdrop closes the menu. What a visitor types is never written back into the page.
- **What it says.** The key help, in words since 2026-10-30: "Up and down to move, Enter to open, Esc to close". Until then it was "↑/↓ move · enter open · esc close", in mono. Copying the address shows "Copied" on a line under the rows, and one `role="status"` region inside the dialog, hidden on screen, repeats it (SC 4.1.3): the page behind a modal dialog is inert, so a region outside it would say nothing. The same region gives the count of rows once typing pauses, "No matches" when none is left, and the theme's new name. When the browser refuses the copy, the line shows the address to copy by hand.
- **Where it stops.** Nothing is stored: the theme entry presses the toggle, whose handler is the only code that reads or writes the stored choice. In forced colors the theme entry is gone, as the toggle is. The page the browser keeps for Back never comes back with the menu open. Paper gets no button.
- **Its cost.** 1,519 B gzip of eager JavaScript on every page, against the research figure of 1.18 KB; its markup adds about 640 to 700 B gzip to each page's HTML, and its styles 468 B to the stylesheet. Checked by `test/palette.test.ts` (the key handler and the matcher, with known answers; on every page of every build, the dialog, its rows against the manifest and the checked profiles, the combobox's wiring, the button), `test/scripts.test.ts` (its module once on every page, in `<body>`, from `/_astro/`) and `e2e/palette.spec.ts` (each key, the button, the narrowing, the arrows, each action, the backdrop, touch and no JavaScript, axe in light, dark and forced colors with it open on every page, no policy violation and no animation, and its fit at 320 px and 32 px text). VoiceOver and NVDA over it are Joseph's to run, as over the toggle ([Accessibility rules](#accessibility-rules)).

## Patterns and further reading

- Mosaic effect (why the coordinates and the UTC offset are gone): US Office of Management and Budget, [Memorandum M-13-13](https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/sites/default/files/omb/memoranda/2013/m-13-13.pdf) (2013).
